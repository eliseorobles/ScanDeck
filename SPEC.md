# ScanDeck — SPEC v0.7 (working title)

A standalone, native macOS 26 batch-scanning app for Apple Silicon in the mold of PaperStream Capture — jobs/profiles, batches, counters, scan/review, and deliberate batch completion — with a modern UI. Designed so its output drops straight into the **carduploader** listing flow for trading-card sellers.

**v1 in one sentence:** pick the job/profile, type a new batch name, feed the cards, glance at the front/back pairs, press Complete Batch — and `scan_000114_F.jpg` / `scan_000114_B.jpg` land in `<output root>/<batch>/`, each image showing the full card or outer holder with configurable real-scanner-background margins (3 mm per edge by default).

---

## 1. Why this exists

- PaperStream Capture (and PaperStream IP) are Windows-only; on macOS, fi-series scanners get a bare ICA driver driven through Apple Image Capture.
- The Mac field is weak for this workflow: VueScan has dated UX and no side-aware naming, ExactScan is a paid general-purpose scanning app rather than a card-batch workflow, and Image Capture has no jobs, naming rules, or completion gate. Apple Communities' best defense of the status quo is that Image Capture is "competent."
- Card sellers specifically need something none of these do: **duplex pairs named by sheet counter and side**, batch folders, and a deliberate review-before-completion step feeding a listing tool.

**Positioning:** not a scanner utility — a production workflow app for high-volume card scanning.

## 2. v1 scope

v1 is done when a seller can run their daily carduploader intake entirely inside ScanDeck:

1. **Scan** front + back of raw cards and cards in toploaders in one duplex ADF pass (600 dpi, 24-bit color).
2. **Full-border output**: each side cropped to the outermost physical item (the raw card or its toploader) plus configurable per-edge margins of real scanner background (black), linked at **3 mm on all four edges by default**. Never synthetic padding — the border is genuine captured background, cropped from a larger frame. Buyers see true edges and centering. Since TICKET-039 that border may be **resampled**: squaring a tilted card interpolates every published pixel from captured neighbours. Nothing is invented and no edge is padded, so the letter holds, but "genuine captured background" now carries one more layer of meaning than it did — the operator accepted that trade explicitly, and §6.2 records the five rules that bound it.
3. **Save** as `scan_<SheetCounter>_<OutputSide>.jpg` — six-digit persistent counter, `F`/`B` side token — into a **batch folder** named by the operator each run.
4. **Review then complete deliberately.** The final batch folder does not appear until the operator presses Complete Batch. No auto-completion in v1.

The v1 media targets are **raw (bare) cards and cards in toploaders** — the operator's real workflow, stated during the first physical runs. The toploader is the primary tested path: real multi-card ADF batches have fed, jammed, recovered through the retry flow, and published. A card in a penny sleeve alone is never scanned. Semi-rigids remain unsupported until they pass physical thickness, transport, duplex, and image-quality testing on the fi-8170; a format that cannot pass safely stays explicitly unsupported rather than being forced through the ADF. (The fi driver's warning that sleeved/thick stock false-triggers ultrasonic multifeed detection is why that feature stays off — a driver fact, not a media promise.)

### The carduploader contract (fixed, not negotiable)
carduploader is an existing, working listing tool; ScanDeck adapts to it, never the reverse:
- JPEG images, front/back as separate files
- Pairing by shared sheet number with `_F` / `_B` suffixes: `scan_000114_F.jpg` + `scan_000114_B.jpg`
- One folder per batch
- Destination root is typically a Google Drive for Desktop folder, so batches upload in the background and are listable from anywhere. ScanDeck treats the destination as a plain folder — no Drive API, ever. ScanDeck guarantees locally atomic publication of the final batch folder; Google Drive may still upload or expose that folder's files incrementally, and cloud-level transactional visibility is outside the contract.

### Non-goals for v1
- Separators (blank page / patch code / barcode), zonal OCR, indexing
- Multi-destination connectors (email/FTP/SharePoint/cloud APIs). The explicit
  post-publication centering check is one narrow derived-output exception: it
  uploads only after a fresh per-run confirmation and is never automatic.
- Flatbed, carrier sheets, front/back merging, cleanup filters (hole-punch removal, edge correction, bleed-through and background cleanup, vertical streak reduction). Deskew was on this list until TICKET-039 measured the tilt and found every card off square; it now ships, on the terms §6.2 records. The rest stay off for reasons that have not changed — each alters captured pixels on an image whose border is the measurement, and the streak filter in particular would silently invalidate the occupancy constants TICKET-019 measured against streaked captures.
- Windows, iPad, Intel Macs; App Store distribution
- Card recognition, pricing, listing intelligence — that's carduploader's side of the line

## 3. The Job model

A **Job** (presented to the operator as a **Profile** tile) = scan settings + processing + naming + destination + counter, stored as JSON in `~/Library/Application Support/ScanDeck/Jobs/`. Ships with one built-in profile, **Cards 600 Full Border**:

| Setting | Value | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| Color mode | 24-bit color | driver request (ICA) |
| Source | document feeder, duplex | driver request |
| Resolution | 600 dpi | driver request |
| Scan window | fixed size comfortably larger than the card or outer holder | driver request — exists only to capture real background for the crop |
| Crop | auto-detect the outermost physical item on black background, accuracy-priority | **app pipeline** |
| Border | top/right/bottom/left real-background margins; linked at 3 mm by default, independently editable | **app pipeline** |
| Rotation | 180° toggle (feed-direction fix) | driver if reachable, else app pipeline pre-encode |
| Blank page skip | Off (backs are often near-blank; never drop them) | driver request — must override driver default On |
| Multifeed detection | Off (sleeved stock false-triggers ultrasonic sensors) | driver request |
| Pre-pick | Off | driver request |
| Filename template | `scan_<SheetCounter>_<OutputSide>.jpg` | app naming engine |
| Counter | six-digit, persistent per job, operator-editable/seedable | app |
| Destination | user-picked folder + required-new per-run batch subfolder | app completion step |
| Encoding | JPEG q95, 600 dpi metadata | app encoder |

Naming engine v1 tokens: literal text, `<SheetCounter>` (zero-padded, width 6), `<OutputSide>` (`F`/`B`). Templates are data, not code — later tokens (date, batch name, job name) slot in without redesign.

## 4. Operator workflow (the whole UI in five beats)

Actual PaperStream Capture is the primary interaction reference for this flow, adapted to native macOS conventions: choose a profile, name the batch, scan and review, continue scanning as needed, then complete the batch.

1. **Job/Profile screen** — big tiles. Click *Cards 600 Full Border*.
2. **Batch prompt** — one text field (`aug01-sale`), Enter to arm. The final `<root>/<batch>/` path must not already exist; an existing folder blocks arming and offers a different name or Show in Finder. A job may have only one active unreleased batch: selecting that job resumes its batch instead of creating another.
3. **Scanning** — the on-screen Scan button starts a feeder pass; the physical scanner button may do the same only after its undocumented fi-driver callback behavior passes a hardware test. Cards appear live as **F/B pairs** (one row per card, front and back side by side, crop preview already applied). Each pair receives a stable provisional counter number when it first appears.
4. **Review / Continue Scanning** — rotate an individual side; delete a side; delete a whole card; or replace a card in place. Deleting one side makes that row an **Incomplete card**. In v1, **Replace Card** always starts a new, isolated one-card duplex scan: ScanDeck must receive and validate both new sides before it atomically swaps both sides into the row, retaining the card's provisional number. Until that swap commits, the existing row is unchanged. There is no UI for choosing one new side, mixing an original side with a replacement side, or replacing only a front or back. **Retry** uses the same isolated duplex operation to turn an incomplete row into a complete pair. A failed or interrupted retry leaves the row incomplete and never shifts later pairs. Whenever any incomplete card exists after a scan operation, both Continue Scanning and Complete Batch are disabled; the operator must either complete it with a successful Retry or remove the entire row with Delete Card. With review clear, Continue Scanning appends another feeder pass to the same batch. Deleting a whole pair leaves a counter gap; later provisional numbers never compact or change.
5. **Complete Batch** — one button. Validate → crop → rotate → encode → name → stage → publish `<root>/<batch>/`; persist the counter; then show a durable summary (cards, counter range, destination, Show in Finder, Check Centering, New Batch). Check Centering is a separate post-publication action: every press confirms the exact image count and names centeringcheck.com before any upload. A failure there cannot undo or fail the completed batch. Unreleased batches survive app restart in the crash-safe session store.

Counter semantics: provisional numbers are stable for the life of an active batch. Completing a batch advances the persistent counter to one past the highest provisional number, so deleted cards can leave intentional gaps. Cancelling the entire batch commits no counter change and burns no numbers.

Failure semantics: an incomplete card blocks both Continue Scanning and Complete Batch until Retry succeeds or Delete Card removes that row. An image without the configured real margin blocks Complete Batch and asks for a retry or settings correction. Any validation, processing, encoding, or staging failure leaves the final batch folder absent, retains the session masters, and does not advance the counter. If interruption occurs after local publication but before counter persistence, the durable release journal finishes the commit during recovery; ScanDeck does not report the batch complete or delete its masters until both steps are durable. A final-folder race or any filename collision refuses to overwrite.

## 5. Output & destination

- Destination root picked via the standard open panel, persisted as a security-scoped bookmark. Never assume a path or drive letter — resolve at pick time.
- A batch name must resolve to a final folder that does not exist both when the batch is armed and immediately before publication. ScanDeck never merges into or overwrites an existing batch folder.
- Complete Batch writes every output into a uniquely named sibling staging directory on the destination volume. After validating and durably flushing all files and the directory, it atomically renames that directory to `<root>/<batch>/`. This makes publication under the final folder name all-or-nothing on the local filesystem.
- A durable release journal bridges destination publication and the job counter, which may live on different volumes and cannot participate in one filesystem transaction. Crash recovery idempotently finishes the recorded rename/counter commit or returns the batch to review without reusing a published range.
- Counter, job state, release journal, and session state use fsync-then-rename discipline.
- Full-resolution session masters are deleted only after publication and counter persistence are both durably recorded as successful. Before that point they remain available for recovery.
- After completion, an explicitly confirmed centering check may add
  `<batch>/centering/` containing only centeringcheck.com's returned overlays,
  a centering-only CSV, cache metadata, and explanatory text. It never moves,
  rewrites, or copies a published ScanDeck JPEG; interruption leaves the batch
  intact and a re-run resumes by content hash.
- Google Drive for Desktop can observe and upload files on its own schedule. ScanDeck does not promise an atomic cloud upload or simultaneous visibility on other devices.

## 6. Technical foundation

### 6.1 Scanning stack (research-confirmed — details in `docs/research/macos-scanning-apis.md`)
- **Primary: Swift + ImageCaptureCore**, verified against the macOS 26 SDK headers and the installed PFU ICA module (v2.4.1, arm64, actively maintained — built April 2025). Session shape: `ICDeviceBrowser` (scanner mask) → `ICScannerDevice.requestOpenSession` → `requestSelect(.documentFeeder)` → configure `{duplexScanningEnabled, resolution=600, pixelDataType=.RGB, bitDepth=.depth8Bits, documentType=.typeA6 or scanArea}` → `requestScan` per feeder pass.
- **Vendor driver options are fully programmable** — no Image Capture pre-configuration needed. `ICScannerFunctionalUnit.vendorFeatures` exposes the fi driver's toggles as public `ICScannerFeature` objects: Cropping (*None (with black background)* ✓), Multifeed detection (*Off* ✓ — driver tooltip itself warns sleeved/thick stock false-triggers), Blank page skip (*Off* ✓), Pre-pick, Scan button monitor, Dropout color, Sharpness, Tone. (Pre-pick defaults *Off* in the built-in job — gentler feeding for sleeved cards — though it costs a little speed.) The app sets these before every scan; jobs own them.
- **Per-page delivery**: `scannerDevice(_:didScanTo:)` fires once per page from the feeder — the natural hook for live pairing, preview, and naming. Rotation: `oddPageOrientation`/`evenPageOrientation` (EXIF orientation, 180° = value 3) on the feeder unit.
- **Duplex pairing**: pages alternate per sheet; pair (2n, 2n+1) with a per-job front-first/back-first switch (no explicit side metadata in ICA). An odd page count or interrupted duplex pass produces an **Incomplete card** row and a Retry prompt. Every Retry or Replace Card operation is a new, isolated one-card duplex pass; it must yield and validate a complete pair before the row changes. Replace Card atomically replaces both sides while retaining the provisional number, with no side-selection or old/new mixing. ScanDeck never infers a missing side or automatically re-pairs later cards. An incomplete row disables Continue Scanning as well as Complete Batch until Retry succeeds or Delete Card removes it.
- **Errors**: multifeed/jam/cover-open arrive as `NSError` (`-9933` unsolicited scanner error, `-9931` failed-to-complete) carrying the driver's own localized strings ("Multifeed detected.", "Paper jammed in the ADF.") — surface them verbatim in the review UI. **One exception, and only one: an empty ADF chute (`0x03800320`, against `0x03800131` for a jam) is not a fault.** It is the normal end of a run — the operator stopped feeding and the next pass found nothing — and it reaches the app as a typed reason at the transport seam rather than as prose, so it can be reported as information ("The feeder is empty… N cards captured.") with the batch intact. The typing happens in `ScanDeckScanner`, which is the layer allowed to know driver codes; the app decides what to show from the case and never from the wording. Every other failure keeps the driver's sentence verbatim.
- **Exclusive session**: ICA scanners are single-client — if Image Capture (or anything) holds the device we get `-9925/-9926`; detect, explain, and auto-resume via `scannerDeviceDidBecomeAvailable`. Each `requestScan` drains the currently loaded hopper; the explicit Continue Scanning action starts another pass in the same active batch when more media is loaded. The live fi-8170 driver can publish a stale `documentLoaded` value, so that property is only a UI hint; the request result and driver error are authoritative.
- **Encode path**: v1 default is file-based transfer (`documentUTI = .jpeg`) — driver-encoded 600 dpi JPEG in, decode → crop → re-encode q95 out (one extra generation, visually lossless at this quality; identical to the proven interim pipeline). Memory-band transfer (raw in, single encode out) is an M1 measurement experiment, adopted only if it shows visible benefit.
- **Free future win**: driverless eSCL/AirScan network scanners surface through the same `ICDeviceBrowser` API — second-hardware support without a second stack.
- **Fallback stack** if the ICA path ever dies: vendored open-source sane-backends (`fujitsu` backend rates the fi-8170 `good`, 04c5:15ff) behind an XPC helper. NAPS2's architecture (ImageCaptureCore-primary, SANE-fallback) independently validates this shape. Explicitly rejected: TWAIN (dead ecosystem), writing our own drivers (ExactScan's multi-year moat), and shipping against PFU's proprietary `libsanepfufs.dylib` (dlopen-able but license-blocked; research-spike curiosity only). NAPS2 itself is GPL-2.0-or-later — reference for QA checklists (its Apple-driver duplex bug history), never a dependency.

### 6.2 Processing pipeline
- Estimate the black background from the frame border; mask foreground at an adaptive threshold, with a doubled-threshold second read as a consensus check
- Bound the dominant connected component by its *supported* rows and columns — occupancy profiles with floors a thin bright streak cannot meet (constants measured on real fi-8170 passes, `CardImageCropper.supportedBounds`) — never by its raw bounding box, which any connected artefact stretches by its own length. **Sub-floor occupancy past a floor bound counts for nothing.** A faint-fringe pass used to extend the bound over any sub-floor tail that died out within a budget, on the theory that a holder's open-end lip fades while an artefact streak runs on; measured across 186 real pairs it caused 23 of the 26 pairs whose two sides disagreed and rescued none, and no occupancy level separates the two cases (TICKET-027). The two-threshold consensus stays: bound movement within `CardImageCropper.maximumEdgeFadePixels` is genuine edge fade (trust the permissive read), movement past it is artefact (trust the conservative read)
- Expand each supported edge by `round(marginMM/25.4 × dpi)` px; the four margins are independently configurable and linked at 3 mm by default (71 px @ 600 dpi)
- **Square the card before cropping it** (TICKET-039, built 2026-07-30 after the operator accepted resampling). Tilt had never been measured; D-06 had declined deskew on "under ~0.5°", a figure that was really an axis-aligned bounding box compared against an assumed 1800 × 2400 holder and so could not separate rotation from a larger holder — nor is it even an upper bound, since a *narrower* holder makes the same arithmetic understate the angle. Measured across 24 real sides from three batches, **every card is off square: 0.19°–1.00°, median 0.54°, zero-mean and random in sign.** What that costs is not cosmetic. The crop is axis-aligned around the bounding box of a tilted card, so the box touches the media at one corner per edge and stands off it everywhere else, and a requested 3.0 mm border therefore publishes as 3.0 mm at that corner and up to 4.8 mm at the far end of the same edge — the configured margin honoured at its minimum and nowhere else. The angle comes from fitting the media's four edges (`MediaTiltEstimator`), never from its bounding box, and the spread across those four edges is the reading's error bar. **The card's size and centre come from those same fitted edges**, for a reason found by looking at real output rather than by reasoning: de-rotating the bounding box instead solves for a rectangle with *sharp* corners, a toploader's are rounded, and the resulting under-measurement published a border of 63–72 px against 71 requested while passing every check that the output was square, uniform and unpadded. All four edges are therefore required — three are enough to agree on an angle but not to bound a rectangle. One consequence is worth stating: the pre-deskew crop was sized from the media's extreme point, so its border was never *below* the requested margin, only above it; a squared crop centres on the requested margin instead and varies with the media edge's own straightness, measured at ±4 px. Five rules make it safe: (1) the tilt is measured **after** detection on the **original** pixels, so TICKET-019's occupancy constants never see a resampled streak; (2) rotation and crop are a single resample, because TICKET-035 removed a double decode and publication does this 800 times a batch; (3) the output rectangle is the media's true footprint plus the requested insets, and `analyze` proves every corner of it lies inside the captured frame *before* anything is drawn — so nothing is padded and the emptied-corner problem that forces PaperStream's own deskew to paint a chosen background colour does not arise; (4) below **0.2°** the image is left byte-identical, because the measured per-side error bar is 0.19° and correcting less than that is fitting noise into every published pixel; (5) past **3.0°** the sheet is misfed rather than tilted and the reading is discarded, as it is when the four edges disagree — in every such case the card publishes exactly as it did before deskew existed, rather than entering a blocking state the reducer has no vocabulary for. `mediaBounds` deliberately still reports the tilt-inflated bounding box, because it is what the 24 px / 140 px pair thresholds were calibrated on and what every stored `AcceptedPairSizeMismatch` was recorded against. Verified on the real corpus rather than on fixtures: 16 of 20 sides corrected, residual median **0.005°**, worst 0.009°, and the four declined were declined by rules (4) and (5).
- If an expanded edge would cross the captured frame, show the available area in review, flag insufficient real margin, and block Complete Batch. Never silently clamp the requested output or synthesize padding; the operator must retry with sufficient capture area or explicitly change the configured margin.
- A card's two sides captured the same physical item, so their measured media footprints must agree within **1 mm (24 px @ 600 dpi)**. Beyond that the pair is flagged on its card row and counted in the action bar; beyond **5.9 mm (140 px @ 600 dpi)** it blocks Complete Batch and asks for a retry. Per-side analysis can be fooled, and both sides read `sufficient` on their own terms when it is, so the pair cross-check is the only signal that anything is wrong at all — it must never become silent, whichever side of the wider line a card falls.
- **The two-threshold split is a deliberate loosening of invariant #2's spirit, and it is recorded as one** (TICKET-033, decided by the operator 2026-07-29). Between 24 px and 140 px ScanDeck now publishes a pair it knows one crop of is wrong. What it buys is that a 4 mm surplus border — an image that still contains the whole card — no longer holds a 400-card batch hostage. What it costs is stated plainly: those files reach `carduploader` with one side cropped differently from the other. The mitigations are not optional and ship with it — the card row says so, the action bar counts them, and the completion screen names the sheet numbers that were released that way. The number came from measurement: with the detector TICKET-027 landed, 186 real pairs leave nothing between 24 px and 95 px, so 140 releases the two survivors whose extra extent is a faint artefact and holds the one where both thresholds agree the back images something 16 mm longer than the front.
- **Past 140 px the operator may overrule the refusal, per card** (TICKET-037, asked for by the operator 2026-07-29 while `a real batch` sat blocked on one card). Complete Batch stays refused until they press Publish Anyway on that card and confirm a message that states both footprints, the millimetres between them, and that one of the two crops is therefore not the real border. Four rules make it an override rather than a hole: (1) the decision is a `BatchSession` action, so the reducer still owns every gate and the presentation model cannot clear a blocker by itself; (2) consent is recorded against the **two measured footprints**, not as a flag, so a Replace that re-measures the pair invalidates it and the gate returns — consent never transfers to numbers nobody was shown; (3) it is withdrawable, from the row it was granted on; (4) the card stays in `pairSizeWarnings` for the rest of the batch's life, so the row badge, the action-bar count and the completion screen all keep saying so, and `CompletionSummary.releasedWithAcceptedMismatches` records it apart from the cards TICKET-033 releases on its own authority. Only a card whose *sole* blocker is the disagreement is overridable; one that is also incomplete or short of real margin stays refused, because a button that visibly does nothing teaches the operator to distrust the control. What it buys is that a batch is never lost to one card — the alternative in practice was deleting it, which is what happened to `a real batch`'s 000188.
- **A published batch is recorded so it can be found again** (TICKET-038). Publication clears the session record once the atomic rename and the counter commit are both durable, which left no trace that a batch had ever existed — so the app could not name one after a relaunch, and the post-publication centering pass was reachable only in the seconds the completion screen was on screen. `PublishedBatchStore` keeps name, card count, sheet range, destination bookmark, and both warning classes; the sidebar lists them and each has Show in Finder and Check Centering. The store is a convenience and is treated as one: an unreadable history reads as empty rather than throwing, because everything in it can be re-derived by looking in the destination folder. Folders already on disk can be imported, and an imported batch **must declare which filename suffix holds the artwork before it can be graded** — ScanDeck publishes artwork in `_F`, batches from before `deliveryOrder: .backFirst` have the card back there, front and back centering tolerances differ by **5×**, and no property of the files can tell them apart. Undeclared refuses; declared inverted passes `--swap-sides`. There is no default, because both candidate defaults report ruinous cards as flawless for half the operator's folders.
- All-black frame → keep full frame, flag in review
- Rotate 180° when the job says so and the driver didn't; encode JPEG q95 with correct DPI metadata
- Implementation: vImage/CoreImage on raw buffers, off the main thread; review grid uses downsampled previews, 600 dpi masters stay in the on-disk session store

### 6.3 App shell
- **Platform v1: macOS 26 on Apple Silicon only.**
- SwiftUI, macOS 26 design language, dark-mode-first; **regular (non-document) app** — NSDocument fights the append-only batch + Complete Batch model. Scanner service wrapped in an actor emitting per-page events via `AsyncStream`.
- Review grid: never bind full-resolution masters — downsample-on-decode (`CGImageSourceCreateThumbnailAtIndex`, ~512 px, `NSCache`) into `LazyVGrid`; masters stay in the on-disk session store. Pipeline work in a bounded `TaskGroup`; final writes via `CGImageDestination` with DPI metadata.
- **Distribution v1: Developer ID + hardened runtime, no App Sandbox** — there's an open report of sandboxed `ICDeviceBrowser` failing to see USB scanners on macOS 15; sandbox/App Store is a later spike, not a v1 bet. Destination access via security-scoped bookmarks either way.
- One active unreleased batch is allowed per job. Provisional counter assignments are stable, pair deletion may leave gaps, and the persistent next counter commits through the release journal at Complete Batch. Cancelling the batch burns no numbers.

## 7. Milestones

- **M0 — Spike (throwaway allowed):** Swift CLI: enumerate the fi-8170 via ICDeviceBrowser, set vendor features programmatically (Cropping=*None (with black background)*, Multifeed=*Off*, Blank skip=*Off*), run one duplex 600 dpi color scan, dump pages to disk. Proves the stack end to end. *Exit: two page files on disk from one card pass, with the vendor settings verifiably applied.* **Met 2026-07-28.**
- **M1 — Headless engine:** job JSON + pipeline + naming + counter + locally atomic batch publication, CLI-triggered. *Exit: one real batch scanned and completed end-to-end from the command line; output accepted by carduploader untouched.* **Met 2026-07-28 (TICKET-006 verification batch).**
- **M2 — The app:** PaperStream-style job/profile tiles, batch prompt, live F/B pair grid, isolated whole-card Retry/Replace, incomplete-card gate on Continue Scanning, review actions, Complete Batch. *Exit: a full real batch without touching a terminal.* **Met 2026-07-28 (the four-card `asd` batch: a jam, a retry, two crash recoveries, mixed holder sizes — published clean; TICKET-008).**
- **M3 — Comfort:** job editor, counter management, anomaly flags, batch history, icon, real name. *Exit: you'd let someone else run a batch.*

Every milestone is validated on Apple Silicon against the physical fi-8170 with the real media — raw cards and cards in toploaders — no simulator-only "done". Semi-rigids receive separate safety and reliability tests before support is claimed.

## 8. Open questions & research

Research is in `docs/research/`:
- `macos-scanning-apis.md` — the ImageCaptureCore facts §6 is built on (verified against the macOS 26 SDK and the installed PFU driver binary)
- `competitor-gaps.md` — positioning: the real competitor is ExactScan Pro (~100€, own drivers), and the two structural gaps nothing on Mac has are the review-then-completion gate and first-class pair naming with transactional counters. VueScan's watermark-trial/renewal model is the goodwill killer to avoid.

A third track — a feature inventory of PaperStream Capture 6.0, distilled from the vendor's 644-page manual — is the source of the post-v1 backlog below (batch state machine, three counter types with persistence modes, naming-token builder with live preview, marks/filters, separators, ad-hoc override, hardware-button job mapping). It is **not published**: it is a dense page-cited abstract of one copyrighted document, and nothing in ScanDeck needs it published to be useful. The backlog items it produced are listed here in their own right.

A separate, broader UX study of a general-purpose PaperStream Capture remake (presets, OCR, searchable PDF, page-list review) exists as local research and is deliberately not published. **It is not this product's roadmap.** Settled 2026-07-28: ScanDeck stays a trading-card app and that document serves as a UX quality bar and post-v1 backlog only. Where the two disagree, this file wins. Consequently the domain models F/B *pairs*, not a general page list; adding a document profile later would be a reducer rewrite, accepted deliberately.

Still open:
1. File-based vs memory-band encode path — decide by measurement in M1 (§6.1).
2. Name. ScanDeck is a placeholder — kept for now; revisit before public release.

Settled: **open source, MIT** (see LICENSE). Distribution of signed builds (Developer ID notarization, maybe paid convenience builds later) stays open until there's something to distribute.
